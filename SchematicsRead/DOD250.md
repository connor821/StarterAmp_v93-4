# DOD 250 Overdrive Preamp (read from the Beavis Audio Research breadboard doc)

Source: `bbp_DOD250Overdrive_Rev_1_1-1.png` and the text extraction `bbp_DOD250Overdrive_Rev_1_1.txt` (beavis audio research, "beavis board project", rev 1.1, 18 Dec 2008). It is a build-your-own breadboard layout with a clean schematic, 2100x1275 scan. The schematic is crisp and every value below is legible; the breadboard layout on the right agrees with it.

The doc itself says the circuit is "almost identical" to the MXR Distortion+, and that it is a mid-70s design using one op-amp followed by a pair of diode clippers in an asymmetric arrangement.

## 1. Complete circuit

### Supply and bias
- 9 V battery +, **R1 100 ohm** in series, then the bias divider: **R2 22k** from the filtered rail to a node, **R3 22k** from that node to ground (so the node sits at about 4.5 V), **C1 10 uF** from the node to ground (bias filter). The 9 V (through R1) also feeds op-amp pin 7.
- **R4 470k** from the 4.5 V bias node to the op-amp non-inverting input (pin 3). So the + input DC-bias source impedance is about 470k.
- The 4.5 V bias point is the signal reference for the whole stage (single-supply op-amp).

### Input
- Input -> **C2 10 nF** -> **R6 10k** -> pin 3 (+) of U1.
- **R5 2.2 Mohm** from the input node (before C2) to ground (input load and DC reference for the jack).
- Input high-pass: C2 10 nF into the effective load at pin 3 (R4 470k in parallel with the op-amp input), corner about 1/(2 pi x 470k x 10n) = 34 Hz. R6 10k with the op-amp input capacitance forms an RF low-pass.

### Op-amp stage
- **U1 = LM741** (original doc part; the doc suggests replacing it with any single op-amp or half a dual, as the 741 is "noisy and not audiophile grade ... just about perfect for an overdrive"). Pin 3 +, pin 2 -, pin 6 out, pin 7 +9 V, pin 4 ground (shown ground at pin 4).
- Non-inverting configuration:
  - Feedback from pin 6 to pin 2: **R8 1 Mohm** in parallel with **C5 22 pF**.
  - Gain leg on pin 2 to ground: **R7 4.7k** in series with **C3 4.7 nF**, in series with **VR1 A500K (GAIN)**. In the schematic VR1's wiper is tied to one end (variable resistor) and the other end goes to ground.
- Gain = 1 + R8 / (R7 + VR1) at frequencies where C3 is a short: from 1 + 1M/(4.7k + 500k) = about **2.98 (9.5 dB)** with the pot at maximum to 1 + 1M/4.7k = about **213 (46.6 dB)** with the pot at 0. The doc notes the original uses a B500K reverse-log pot, and says the A500K choice "won't make a huge difference in tone".
- C3 4.7 nF sets a gain-leg corner of 1/(2 pi x (4.7k + VR1) x 4.7n): about **7.2 kHz** with the pot at 0, falling to about 68 Hz with the pot at 500k. So at high gain the stage amplifies mostly treble and the lows are only at unity-ish gain. This corner moves with the pot.
- C5 22 pF with R8 1M gives a feedback pole near 7.2 kHz (1/(2 pi x 1M x 22p)) at full gain: "tames a bit of high-end in the feedback loop (the loop between pins 6 and 2). It also helps keep the circuit from oscillating."
- LM741 gain-bandwidth is about 1 MHz, so at the maximum gain of 213 the small-signal bandwidth is only about 4.7 kHz, and the 741's slew rate (about 0.5 V/us) limits large high-frequency signals. This is an observation from the part's standard datasheet, not from the doc.

### Output coupling and clipper
- Pin 6 -> **C4 4.7 uF** (series coupling) -> **R9 10k** (series) -> clipping node.
- At the clipping node, to ground:
  - **D1 1N4148**: one diode, anode toward the node, cathode to ground (the symbol points down to ground). This clips the positive swing at about +0.6 V.
  - **D2 and D3, both 1N4148, in series with each other**, with both oriented the opposite way (cathode toward the node, anode toward ground; both symbols point up). This clips the negative swing at about -1.2 V (two diode drops).
  - So the clipper is **asymmetric: one diode on the positive side, two in series on the negative side** (the doc: "a pair of diode clippers in asymmetric arrangement"). Diodes are all **1N4148 (silicon)**.
  - **C6 1 nF** from the clipping node to ground (treble roll-off, forms a low-pass with R9 10k: about 16 kHz; with the diodes conducting it acts on the clipped signal).
- Then the **VR2 A100K (VOLUME)** pot from the clipping node to ground, wiper = Output. The output is therefore the clipped signal at the end of R9, scaled by the pot.

### Output
- Wiper of VR2 is the OUT jack. There is no output buffer: the output impedance is the R9 plus the pot setting (up to roughly 35k at mid-rotation, 10k at full volume).

## 2. Gain range summary

| Gain pot (VR1) | Stage gain | dB | Gain-leg corner (C3 with R7+VR1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 213 | 46.6 | 7.2 kHz |
| 50k | about 19.3 | 25.7 | about 620 Hz |
| 500k (max) | about 2.98 | 9.5 | about 68 Hz |

(The table uses the pot position values directly and the formula 1 + 1M/(4.7k + VR1); the pot direction on the knob, which end of the pot is clockwise, is not stated in the doc.)

The stage is a **hard-clipped** design: the maximum gain is followed by diode clipping at the 0.6 V and 1.2 V levels, so the output of the clipper is roughly -1.2 V to +0.6 V around its bias, regardless of the gain, before the volume pot.

## 3. Tone and level circuit

There is **no tone control** in this pedal. The tone is set by: the C3 4.7 nF gain-leg high-pass (which moves with the gain pot), the C5 22 pF feedback pole, the C6 1 nF clipper-node low-pass, and the C4 4.7 uF + volume pot output high-pass. The only level control is VR2 A100K.

## 5. Doc's own mod notes
- Try different values for D1, D2 and D3, different diode types, LEDs.
- Try other single op-amps or half of a dual.
- The original uses a **B500K** reverse-log gain pot (the doc's pot is an A500K).
- Try other values for the 22 pF cap.

## 6. Things I could not confirm
- The breadboard and the schematic agree. The only thing the schematic does not state is the polarity of the electrolytics C1 and C4 (the breadboard picture shows + on the 10 uF and 4.7 uF caps; C4's + is toward the op-amp output side as expected **(?)**).
- Whether the 741's pin 7 supply is the R1-filtered rail (the schematic draws it from the R1 side).
