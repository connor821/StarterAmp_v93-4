### What matters for the simulator

The U 87 is not simply an SM57 with more treble. Its capsule, polar pattern, off-axis behavior and distance response all contribute to the result. It is also a credible real-world guitar-cab microphone: Sweetwater's controlled guitar-cab shootout included the U 87 Ai alongside an SM57, R-121, M 160, MD 421-II and C414 XLS. InSync +1

I'd implement these separate response states:

* Cardioid, flat filter

* Cardioid, low-frequency roll-off

* Omni, flat filter

* Figure-eight, flat filter

Keep the −10 dB pad as an input-headroom setting rather than a tonal EQ curve, unless measurements show a meaningful response difference.

Important limitation: the manufacturer's published U 87 documentation provides response specifications and graphs, but I have not verified a complete measured set of U 87-specific proximity curves at several distances. Don't label synthetic curves as manufacturer measurements.

### 8\. One important DSP recommendation

I'd separate the mic model into three components:

* Intrinsic mic response: the measured frequency-response curve, including its presence peak and high-frequency roll-off.

* Distance response: measured proximity effect and distance-dependent spectral changes, where actual data is available.

* Angle and position response: off-axis coloration, capsule orientation and speaker-cone position.

That last component matters enormously for guitar. Moving a mic from the dust-cap center toward the cone edge can change brightness dramatically, even without changing the microphone or its distance. The Audix i5 documentation explicitly discusses this positioning effect. cdn.audixusa.com +1

For a release aimed at progressive metal and djent, my recommended expanded core library would be the SM57, SM7B, MD 421-II, e906, M 160, M 201, R-121, R-10, U 87 Ai, C414 XLS, C414 XLII, Audix i5 and RE20.

That provides dynamic, ribbon and condenser options without requiring you to build dozens of poorly sourced mic models. I'd rather have 12 well-validated models—with measured responses, distance behavior where supported, and sensible interpolation—than 30 models made from generic EQ curves.
